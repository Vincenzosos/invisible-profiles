\chaptertitlepage{The Italian Silver Atlas}{An operational instrument for the social-mediation layer}
\label{ch:atlas}

\section{Overview}
\label{sec:atlas-overview}

The substantive analysis of Chapters~\ref{ch:ch4}--\ref{ch:ch7} identifies a single dominant innovation case for the Italian silver economy: the Moderate Isolated segment (26.9\% of the analytical sample) is autonomous and non-trivially digital yet disengaged from the formal healthcare system, and its binding constraint is the absence of a social-and-informational mediation layer rather than the absence of a digital channel (\S\ref{sec:ch6-product-design-gap}). This chapter documents the operational counterpart of that argument: the \emph{Italian Silver Atlas} (\url{https://invisible-profiles.vercel.app}), a companion platform built as a working prototype of the mediation interface that the Italian welfare regime does not currently provide.

The platform is presented here as an operational artefact and design proposition, not as a tested intervention; empirical validation through user-comprehension studies and B2B field-trials is set out as a future-research direction in \S\ref{sec:ch9-future}. The chapter first describes what the platform delivers and how an organisation uses it (\S\ref{sec:atlas-delivers}--\S\ref{sec:atlas-action}), and then summarises the statistical machinery that lets the typology be transported to an organisational cohort under explicit uncertainty (\S\ref{sec:atlas-scoring}).

\section{What the platform delivers}
\label{sec:atlas-delivers}

The Atlas has two faces. The public face surfaces the academic findings as navigable content --- a per-profile passport page for each archetype (descriptive signature, matched-pair gap, welfare-state translation) and a methodology section mirroring Chapters~\ref{ch:ch3} and~\ref{ch:ch7}. This presentation layer makes the typology legible, but it is not where the operational contribution lies.

The operational core is a business-to-business cohort-segmentation interface for organisations that hold a senior-population dataset --- the platform names healthcare-practice managers, pharmacy-chain analysts and silver-economy product managers as its intended users. The interface assigns each record in the organisation's cohort to one of the Italian (or Swedish) archetypes, reports the cohort's segment composition against the national SHARE baseline, and surfaces the share of the cohort falling into the country-specific configurations identified in the thesis. The instrument occupies a position that population-level segmentation tools (Eurostat, Eurobarometer), commercial silver-economy reports segmenting by age and income alone \citep{vuik2016}, and clinical risk scores do not: a cohort-screening instrument that operationalises a multidimensional academic typology of late-life experience.

\section{The B2B cohort workflow}
\label{sec:atlas-workflow}

The user uploads a CSV with one row per individual and maps the available columns to the ten profiler variables through a guided wizard (Figure~\ref{fig:atlas-wizard}). Mappings are auto-detected by name match, fuzzy similarity and value range, with a confidence label beside each suggestion; each profiler variable is presented as a plain-language question with its SHARE coding range and a \emph{typical company-DB proxy} hint (for example, internet use proxied by application-login frequency, financial distress by late-payment count or credit score, quality of life by an NPS or brand-affinity index). A coverage meter reports how many of the ten variables are mapped --- the scoring requires a minimum of three, and unmapped dimensions are simply left out of the subspace rather than defaulted.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/webapp/webapp_06_mapping_wizard}
  \caption[Italian Silver Atlas, column-mapping wizard]{Column-mapping wizard. Each of the ten profiler variables is presented as a plain-language question with its SHARE coding range, an auto-detected mapping with a confidence label, a typical company-database proxy, and an optional rank-quantile rescaling control. The coverage meter (here ten of ten) governs how many dimensions enter the scoring subspace.}
  \label{fig:atlas-wizard}
\end{figure}

Before scoring, two safeguards are exposed. A missing-value policy lets the user skip incomplete rows (the conservative default) or impute from the cohort's own observed distribution. A \emph{sample-skew check} runs a per-variable two-sample test of the cohort against the SHARE Wave~9 reference and flags, in a non-blocking amber panel, any variable whose distribution departs materially from it --- making the boundary of validity visible rather than enforcing it silently (\S\ref{sec:atlas-scoring}).

Scoring produces the cohort dashboard (Figure~\ref{fig:atlas-dashboard}). Four headline cards report the cohort size, the \emph{Isolation Paradox flag} (the share assigned to the Moderate Isolated configuration of \S\ref{sec:ch4-isolation-paradox}), the digitally-addressable share, and the intermediated-need share, each against its national delta. A statistical-fit line reports a Pearson $\chi^2$ test of the cohort distribution against the national baseline, and a cluster-distribution chart plots the cohort share against the SHARE Wave~9 baseline for every archetype.

\section{From cohort to action}
\label{sec:atlas-action}

Clicking any archetype opens a cluster dossier (Figure~\ref{fig:atlas-drilldown}) that turns the segment share into an operational brief: a short ``what this cluster makes visible'' narrative, a per-record confidence summary, the cohort-versus-SHARE signature across all ten variables, the cohort's demographic composition, and the cluster's national-level healthcare-engagement gaps. A complementary per-record table (Figure~\ref{fig:atlas-perrow}) reports, for each individual, the assigned profile, an evidence label, the top-two profile probabilities, the distance percentile, and the cross-country twin.

The three intended user roles map onto three uses of this output. A healthcare-practice manager reads the Isolation Paradox flag and the dossier's healthcare-engagement gaps to identify the autonomous-but-disengaged patients who slip through standard triage, and targets them with outreach rather than clinical escalation. A pharmacy-chain analyst reads the digitally-addressable split to decide which segments a direct-to-consumer digital service can reach and which require an intermediated channel. A silver-economy product manager reads the aggregate metrics --- in the test cohort, a digitally non-addressable share and a social-mediation-layer-required share of roughly two thirds --- as the market-sizing of the design problem the thesis identifies. In each case the deliverable is the same: a screening instrument that routes a heterogeneous senior cohort to the design logic its empirical signature implies.

\section{How records are scored}
\label{sec:atlas-scoring}

Scoring operates on the ten profiler variables in the within-country standardised ($z$) space in which the K-means centroids of Chapters~\ref{ch:ch4} and~\ref{ch:ch5} are defined; a record with complete coverage is assigned to the nearest centroid in Euclidean distance. Four devices handle the ways an organisational cohort departs from the clean SHARE sample, and they share one design principle: each makes the uncertainty of a transported assignment visible rather than hiding it behind a point estimate.

\emph{Rank-quantile rescaling} maps a proxy column on an arbitrary operational scale onto the SHARE coding through a monotone quantile transform, transporting the cohort's rank information without assuming a parametric proxy-to-target relationship. \emph{Conditional-Gaussian imputation} handles partial coverage: modelling the standardised vector as $z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \Sigma)$ with $\Sigma$ the country's empirical correlation matrix from SHARE Wave~9, the missing coordinates $M$ given the observed coordinates $O$ follow a Gaussian with conditional mean $\mu_{M\mid O} = \Sigma_{MO}\,\Sigma_{OO}^{-1} z_O$ and the corresponding Schur-complement covariance. Rather than collapse this to its mean, the platform draws $M = 100$ completions (by Cholesky factorisation of the conditional covariance), classifies each to its nearest centroid, and returns the modal cluster with the share of draws as its probability --- so a record near a boundary returns a genuine distribution over profiles rather than a spurious point assignment. The \emph{Kolmogorov--Smirnov pre-flight} (Figure~\ref{fig:atlas-ks}) compares each mapped variable's cohort distribution to the SHARE reference, $D = \sup_x |F_{\text{cohort}}(x) - F_{\text{SHARE}}(x)|$, and flags material departures. The \emph{evidence ladder} reports a per-record label on a $0$--$10$ scale that adds a coverage component to a decisiveness component (the top-one probability and its margin over the runner-up), so a sharp peak resting on thin coverage is capped below the ``Strong'' band rather than over-trusted.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{figures/webapp/webapp_07_sample_skew_ks}
  \caption[Italian Silver Atlas, sample-skew pre-flight]{Sample-skew pre-flight. A non-blocking panel reports, for each mapped variable, a two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov comparison of the cohort against the SHARE Wave~9 reference, flagging the dimensions on which the transported assignment is least reliable.}
  \label{fig:atlas-ks}
\end{figure}

The platform is honest about what it does not establish: the typology is descriptive of the SHARE sample partition (\S\ref{sec:ch3-preprocessing}), the proxy mappings transport rank rather than construct validity, and the cohort assignments are provisional screening signals rather than diagnoses. So framed, the Atlas is the operational instrument through which the central finding of the thesis --- that the Italian silver-economy gap is a missing social-and-informational mediation layer, not a missing digital channel (\S\ref{sec:ch6-product-design-gap}) --- is made usable by the organisations positioned to close it. The platform is currently supported analytically and untested in field; the two validation routes, a qualitative comprehension study and a B2B field-trial, are developed in \S\ref{sec:ch9-future}.
